David Carson changed what typography could be. Before him, type
existed to be read. After him, type could be felt — layered,
broken, buried, and reborn as something you experience before you
parse. This project was a study of his work and his philosophy,
built as a complete editorial booklet that tried to incorporate his
concepts into my own design language.

## The approach

Carson's work in *Ray Gun* and *Beach Culture* threw out the rule
book. Grids were suggestions, not laws. Text could run sideways,
overlap, disappear into an image, or sit at a size that made it
impossible to read but impossible to ignore. I tried to work the
same way — not copying his layouts, but adopting his mindset. What
happens when the grid breaks? What happens when type stops serving
the reader and starts serving the page?

[[03-spread-1.jpg]]

## Deconstruction

The interior spreads layer text at multiple scales, weights, and
opacities. Some text is readable. Some is texture. Some is both,
depending on how close you stand. The hierarchy isn't top-down —
it's spatial. What you read first depends on where your eye lands,
and your eye lands wherever the contrast is loudest.

[[04-spread-2.jpg]]

## Image and type

Carson never treated photographs as containers for captions. Image
and type occupied the same plane, each one cutting into the other.
I worked the same way here — text set into the photograph, not on
top of it, and imagery cropped to meet the type where it lives.

[[05-spread-3.jpg]]

## Layering

Some spreads run four or five text layers at once — body copy,
pull quotes, folios, and decorative type all occupying the same
space. The result is visual noise that resolves into meaning if you
slow down and let your eyes adjust. It's not chaos. It's density.

[[06-spread-4.jpg]]

## Closing

The final spread pulls back. Still fractured, still Carson, but
quieter — the noise fading to a signal. The booklet was built in
InDesign with heavy use of text wrapping, custom frames, and
manual placement. No grid held it together. The page itself was the
only constraint.
