Rain City Brew was putting a porter into bottles and needed packaging for
it. Troy Lancaster, the owner and brewmaster, told me what he wanted the
beer to feel like:

> Porter is a dark and hearty, warming brew. We want something that makes
> you feel like you're coming in out of the cold to warm up next to a fire
> and have a nice beer. Think lighthouses and stained glass windows.

## The label

So I drew a white lighthouse against a black sky, with orange and yellow
beams coming off it through the clouds over Puget Sound. The beams handle
the stained glass part of the brief. They also give the label one thing to
look at from across a shop, which is about all you get on a shelf.

[[02-label.png]]

I set it up as a real print file rather than a nice-looking comp. The
government warning, the state-by-state deposit list, the barcode, the fluid
ounces, the alcohol content. All of that takes up room. Leave it to the end
and you spend the last hour of the job wrecking the front panel to make it
fit.

## Printed

Then I printed the artwork, put the carrier together and labeled bottles.
That's the only way to find out whether a wrap lines up at the seams and
whether the design still reads once it's curved around glass.

[[01-sixpack.jpg]]

This was a school project. Rain City took me on as their design intern
after they saw it.
