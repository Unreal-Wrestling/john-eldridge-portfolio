Rain City Brew needed packaging that placed the beer unmistakably in the
Pacific Northwest, for a porter the brewmaster described as "dark and
hearty" and meant to be drunk coming in out of the cold.

## The concept

The brief from owner and brewmaster Troy Lancaster pointed at lighthouses
and stained glass windows. I built the label around a white lighthouse
against a dark, stormy sky, with orange and yellow beams radiating out
through wispy clouds over the Puget Sound.

The beam treatment does two jobs at once: it reads as stained glass at a
glance, and it gives the label a strong focal point that stays legible from
across a shelf, which matters more than detail at retail distance.

[[02-label.png]]

The label was built as a real print file rather than a presentation
comp — government warning text, the state-by-state deposit panel, the
barcode and the fluid-ounce and ABV statements are all set in place, because
those elements take up space a design has to accommodate from the start.

## Off the screen

The artwork was printed and assembled into a finished six-pack carrier and
applied to bottles, which is the only way to find out whether a wraparound
design actually lines up at the seams and reads on a curve.

[[01-sixpack.jpg]]

The result matches the beer: bold, warm, and specific to the region it
comes from. The brewery took me on as a design intern off the back of this
project.
