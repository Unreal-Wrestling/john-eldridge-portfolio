Blank You Very Much was a contest-based platform that paired major
brands with creatives through open design competitions. The format was
straightforward: a brand posted a brief — apparel graphics, packaging
designs, brand collateral — and designers across the country submitted
work. The brand picked a winner and awarded the contract. Everyone
else got a ranking, feedback, and portfolio pieces.

It was speculative work, but it was real work. Each brief came with
brand guidelines, templates, and assets — the same starting point
every contestant got. The constraint was the brand's existing identity;
the challenge was pushing it somewhere new without breaking it. I
entered four contests between August 2014 and April 2015, each a
different brand in a different medium: streetwear apparel, vaporizer
packaging, furniture brand design, and motorsport apparel. None of
them won the contract. I placed highly in all of them.

The value was in the range. Four brands, four design problems, four
sets of constraints — and twenty pieces that held their own against a
national field of working creatives.

## Asphalt Yacht Club

Asphalt Yacht Club was a streetwear brand built on a nautical-meets-
asphalt aesthetic — sailing iconography filtered through a motorsport
lens. The contest brief called for tee shirt graphics that fit the
brand's identity. I submitted seven designs, each taking the
nautical-asphalt premise in a different direction.

The anchor and compass rose piece was the most direct read of the
brand — maritime symbolism rendered as a streetwear emblem. The yacht
silhouette over asphalt texture leaned into the contrast at the brand's
core. The Eye of Horus woven into nautical rope knots pushed the
furthest from the brief, mixing Egyptian iconography into the maritime
vocabulary. The cloud-sky field with a small sailboat was the quietest
submission — negative space doing most of the work. The skull and
crossbones with rigging elements was the most aggressive. The graffiti
spray-paint abstract with fragmented nautical shapes was the loosest,
letting the texture carry the concept. And the small crest-and-anchor
emblem on chest was the restrained entry — the brand mark carrying the
shirt with nothing else competing.

## Grenco G Pen Pro

Grenco Science manufactured the G Pen Pro, a dry herb vaporizer aimed
at a lifestyle market. The contest brief called for packaging and
product concept designs — retail-ready box cuts and visual concepts
that positioned the device beyond its function.

I submitted three packaging dielines and three concept mockups. The
dielines were built in Illustrator as flattened box layouts — print-
ready files with product windows, brand panels, and color systems in
black and green, the brand's core palette. Each one approached the
unboxing experience differently: one led with the product window,
another with lifestyle photography integrated into the panel design,
and a third compressed the layout into a more compact retail footprint.

The concept pieces were built in Photoshop as presentation mockups —
the vaporizer staged in dark environments with green brand lighting,
lifestyle context that sold the product as a design object rather than
a device. The final concept board pulled everything together: multiple
product views, brand elements, and lifestyle context in a single
presentation layout.

## Modernica

Modernica is a Los Angeles furniture manufacturer best known for the
fiberglass Eames shell chair — the mid-century classic they still
produce using the original presses. The contest brief called for brand
designs that connected the furniture to a broader lifestyle context,
positioning it beyond the product itself.

I submitted one design that went through a clear iteration. The early
version placed the chair as a design object in a photographic setting
with the Modernica wordmark — the furniture as something you build a
room around. The final version refined the layout, pushing the brand
identity past the product shot and into something that felt like a
lifestyle campaign.

## Red Bull Global Rallycross

Red Bull Global Rallycross was a motorsport series — modified cars
racing on mixed-surface circuits with dirt jumps and tarmac sections.
The contest brief called for tee shirt designs that could sell at
events and online, plus supporting graphics.

I submitted four shirt designs and a rally course map. The shirts were
built in Illustrator for the vector graphics — checkered flag motifs,
series branding, and racing iconography — and mocked up in Photoshop
for presentation. The red-ground tee with the checkered flag was the
boldest color entry; the dark-ground pieces let the racing graphics
and GRC logo treatment carry the weight. Each shirt took a different
angle on the motorsport theme while staying inside Red Bull's visual
world.

The course map was a separate piece — a stylized diagram of a
rallycross circuit built in Illustrator, with sector markers, track
layout, and series branding. It was the one piece in the set that was
purely informational design, and it rounded out the submission with
something that could work as event signage or print collateral beyond
apparel.
