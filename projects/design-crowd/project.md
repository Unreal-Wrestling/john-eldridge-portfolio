Design Crowd was a contest-based platform that connected clients with
creatives through open design competitions. The format was the same as
Blank You Very Much: a client posted a brief, designers submitted work,
and the client picked a winner. The difference was scope — Design
Crowd ran logo contests for small businesses and startups, not brand
campaigns for major labels. Each brief was a single mark: a company's
entire identity compressed into one readable symbol.

I submitted work for over twenty clients between 2013 and 2014. The
briefs ranged from a legal services firm that wanted a quill pen to a
honey brand that needed an artisan feel to a medical cannabis
dispensary looking for a leaf-and-cross mark. Some came with reference
images and competitor analysis. Some came with a name and a sentence.
The constraint was always the same: the logo had to read at business
card size and billboard size, in one color and full color, on a
website and on a delivery truck.

I didn't win any of the contests. But the range was the point — over
twenty identity systems across industries that had nothing in common,
each one a different design problem with a different set of constraints.

## Logos

The bulk of the work was individual logo marks. Each one started from
the client's brief — their name, their industry, whatever visual
direction they provided — and ended as a finished mark built in
Illustrator.

The EF monogram was a clean interlocking letter treatment for a
professional services firm. Xtreme was an aggressive angular wordmark
for an extreme sports brand. The Whit Gym logo put a barbell inside a
circular badge for a fitness brand. Resonance used concentric sound
wave rings for an audio company. Velvet Honey paired a honey dipper
illustration with elegant serif typography for an artisan food brand.
Peninsula used a flamingo standing in shallow water for a hospitality
destination. Simply Probate took a quill pen writing in ink for a
legal services firm. Traemand built a stylized tree with exposed roots
for an environmental brand. Greenlife integrated a cannabis leaf into
a medical cross for a dispensary. On Your Marks combined a racing
starter pistol with track elements for a motorsport event.

## Brand Presentations

Some contests rewarded presentation as much as the mark itself. These
submissions went beyond a flat logo file and included mockups,
application examples, and multi-page identity boards.

IQra was an educational platform that needed a full identity system —
the logo, an open book with a diagonal slash element, came with a
seven-page presentation showing the mark across touchpoints. Otto was
a stencil-cut wordmark in industrial lettering, presented with a mockup
showing the mark applied to a physical surface. SAEM used a circular
emblem with academic seal treatment for a professional association.
SARA built a surveyor's compass rose with crosshair detailing for a
land surveying brand. Saber formed a sword blade into the vertical
stroke of the letter S, presented with an apparel mockup showing the
mark on a dark garment. Store2Door combined a house and arrow
suggesting delivery from retail to home, presented with a vehicle
mockup showing the brand in its operational context.

## Apparel & Print

Two contests fell outside the logo format. The Triathlon Series was an
event identity — a logo combining swimming, cycling, and running
iconography for a race series, presented on a technical tee mockup. The
button cover was a decorative print piece — a textured surface design
for a fashion accessory, built in Illustrator and Photoshop with
physical production in mind.
