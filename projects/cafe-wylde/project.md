Cafe Wylde is a vegan restaurant in Everett, Washington. A friend was
opening the place and needed a logo — something that felt wild and
organic, not corporate or clinical. The name itself suggested the
direction: untamed, natural, a little feral. The brief was a
hand-lettered wordmark that could carry the brand on its own without
a symbol or badge.

The challenge was finding the right balance between "wild" and
"legible." A vegan restaurant needs to read as approachable and
appetizing, not rough or unfinished. The logo had to work on a
storefront sign, a menu, a social media profile, and a business
card — so it needed to scale cleanly from large format down to a
thumbnail.

I started by exploring seventeen hand-lettered and script fonts,
pulling reference from font libraries to understand the range of
what was possible. Blowbrush, Docktrin, The Dead Saloon, Vanilla
Whale — each had qualities worth borrowing but none was right on its
own. The solution was going to be custom lettering, not an off-
the-shelf typeface.

The first concepts were serif-based and decorative. Leaf motifs,
flourishes, sprout illustrations — they were too literal. The
restaurant didn't need a plant on the logo to communicate "vegan."
The wordmark itself needed to do the work. By concept six I had
moved to hand-lettered forms with an uneven baseline, testing
whether imperfection could feel intentional rather than sloppy. It
could. That direction held.

The remaining iterations refined the lettering — stroke
consistency, weight balance between "Cafe" and "Wylde," spacing, and
the personality of individual characters. The "W" in "Wylde" became
the signature: slightly wilder than the rest of the wordmark, with
a wider stance and more expressive strokes. It gave the logo its
character without breaking legibility.

The final logo was delivered as a vector EPS, print-ready PDF, and
web-optimized PNG. Twelve concepts over ten days, May 22 through
June 2, 2016.
