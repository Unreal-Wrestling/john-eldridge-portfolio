Arisen From Nothing were a rock band out of the Pacific Northwest. I
worked with them from 2011 through 2016 under my own studio, Inkboard
Design — shooting their live shows, running press and promo sessions,
designing their logotype, their show posters and the sleeve for their
EP, and mixing two tracks on that record.

Most of it was unpaid. I took it on to build a body of music-industry
work and to open doors with other bands, with the idea that it might
lead to a larger record design project. That never materialised.

The run was not evenly spread. The bulk of it lands between 2011 and
2013 — a dense stretch of bar shows, festival dates, a summer tour and
the release night — then the design work carries into 2014 with the EP,
and there is a single reunion date in March 2016. The training was in
the density, not the duration: enough shows close together that I
stopped thinking about the camera.

What follows is a selection, not the archive. Several nights I shot are
not represented here yet.

Two pieces of artwork recur in these photographs that I did not make.
The band already had an engraved crest — Surrectum Ex Nihilum, In
Nothing We Trust — applied to the bass drum head and an amp cabinet
before I arrived. The skull-and-revolvers emblem on the EP front was
supplied to me to build around. The photographs are mine and the sleeve
layout is mine. Those two marks are theirs.

## Live Music Photography

Hempfest, the Hard Rock Cafe, Studio 7 and Flight's.

Outdoor festival daylight at one end of the range, small rooms running
whatever the house rig happened to be at the other. Access varied just
as much — sometimes a pit, sometimes wherever there was space to stand.
The job never changed: work with the light that is already in the room,
because you do not get to adjust it and you do not get a second take.

## CD Release Show

The night In Nothing We Trust came out, the band played Hailey's. I
covered the whole day rather than just the set, which is where the
useful pictures usually are — load-in, an empty room at soundcheck,
gear staged against a wall, and the merch table laid out before anyone
was let in.

## Press & Promo Photography

The live coverage was half the job. The band also needed images they
could hand to venues, put in a media kit, and run online. That produced
several sets — a press selection, a black and white series against a
bare wall with a variant cut for folio use, a colour take from the same
session, and a group of later selects.

Studio 7 was the one venue where the portraits mattered more than the
set. Its loading door and sticker wall made a better backdrop than
anything I could have built.

## Design & Mixing

The logotype saw more use than anything else I made for them. The bass
player already used an anarchy symbol, so I built the wordmark around it
instead of replacing it — ARISEN FROM NOTHING set in a spray-spatter
treatment with the anarchy A leading the line. I drew it in black and
white so it would sit on either background.

[[afn-logo.png]]

It had to survive being reproduced badly. A one-colour screen print at
chest width was the hardest test, so that is the one I mocked up first.

[[mockup-shirt.png]]

The EP came in 2014. I designed the sleeve as a single wrap, front and
back panel together: the tracklist, the credits, the ghosted song titles
running behind the type, and the cracked ground the whole thing sits on.
The emblem at the centre was supplied by the band and I built the layout
around it. Inkboard Design is credited on the back panel.

I also mixed and mastered two tracks on that release — the Indigo Mix
versions of Bring the War and In Nothing We Trust. TJ Hill recorded,
mixed and mastered the rest of the record.

### Show posters

Three posters over 2013. They are the pieces of this job that came with
real constraints: a fixed date, a venue's own branding to sit alongside,
support acts to rank, sponsors to place, and a reader who is looking at
it for about a second and a half on the way past.

The Hard Rock Cafe date had the most supplied material to absorb — the
venue's logo, three support-act marks and a ticketing mark, none of them
designed to live together. I built the layout around the band photograph
and let the treated brick carry the rest.

[[poster-hardrock.jpg]]

The Summer Inquisition tour poster had the opposite problem: five dates
and five venues to make legible, with no room for a hierarchy trick. I
staggered the dates left and right down the sheet so the eye walks the
itinerary instead of reading a table, and set the sponsor marks in a
single band along the foot.

[[poster-tour-2013.jpg]]

The Louie G's Pizza date in November went the other way again — a
crowd frame as the whole background, a chiselled wordmark rather than
the spray logotype, and the band's own crest ghosted behind it.

[[poster-louie-gs.jpg]]
