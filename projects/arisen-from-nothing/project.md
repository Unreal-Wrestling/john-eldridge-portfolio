Arisen From Nothing was a rock band based in the Pacific Northwest. I
worked with them from 2012 through 2016 as their photographer and
designer, covering live shows, shooting press and promo material, and
producing all the graphic design the band needed — CD artwork, event
posters, album covers, and their logo treatment.

The relationship started with live music photography. I shot shows at
six venues over four years: Hempfest, Hard Rock Cafe, Studio 7,
Flight's, a Firefighter Benefit concert, and Evergreen SF. Each venue
had its own lighting conditions, its own constraints, and its own
access situation. Some gave me a photo pit. Some put me behind the
bar. The work was the same every time — capture the energy of a live
performance in conditions I could not control.

Beyond the live work, the band needed press and promo material. I ran
studio shoots with controlled lighting, producing both color and black
and white treatments. The black and white series went through multiple
iterations — folio variants with heavier grain and tighter crops for
print layouts. A separate set of final images, the Eldridge series,
combined stage performance with environmental portraiture using gel
lighting and golden hour fill.

The design work grew out of the photography. The band needed CD
release artwork across multiple panels — front cover, inside spread,
back cover, disc face, and booklet. I designed event posters for
Rockfest and other shows, each with its own visual treatment. The
Nether album required a full package: cover, interior panels, and back
matter with a consistent atmospheric motif running through all three
pieces. I also produced the band's logo treatment in white for use on
dark backgrounds.

The work spanned four years and three release cycles. Everything the
band used — every press shot, every piece of CD artwork, every event
poster — came from this relationship.

## Live Music Photography

Six venues, four years. Each one was a different problem.

Hempfest was an outdoor festival — full daylight, no stage lighting to
speak of, and a crowd that pressed against the barrier. The shots had
to work with sun flare and motion blur, not against them. Hard Rock
Cafe had a proper stage with gel lighting, but the room was small and
the angles were tight. Studio 7 gave me the most dynamic lighting —
purple and blue gels that turned the stage into something otherworldly.
Flight's 12-29 was a year-end show with green and amber gels and a
crowd that wanted to be part of the performance. The Firefighter
Benefit was a charity event with white stage lights and an American
flag backdrop — straightforward coverage with no room to get clever.
Evergreen SF was the last show I shot, and the lighting was the most
challenging: strobes that froze some frames and washed out others.

## Promo/Press Photography

The live work was half the job. The band also needed press shots for
venue bookings, media kits, and online presence. I ran two separate
promo shoots and one final series.

The first set — eight press images — covered the range the band
needed: full group shots, individual portraits, and candid moments.
Some were lit with studio strobes. Some used available light. The
black and white series was a separate shoot with its own treatment:
deep shadows, heavy contrast, and a folio variant with tighter crops
and more grain for print layouts. The color promo paired the vocalist
against a brick wall with mixed tungsten and daylight. The Eldridge
finals combined stage performance with environmental portraiture —
colored gels on one, golden hour fill on the other.

## Graphic Design / Branding

The design work started with the CD release. The band needed full
packaging — front cover, inside panel, back cover, disc face, and
booklet spread. I built the system around a dark, textured aesthetic
with distressed type and performance photography integrated into the
layout. The disc face used a circular layout with the logo centered and
track numbers radiating outward. The booklet spread ran a full-bleed
live photo with lyrics overlaid in a handwritten style.

The Rockfest posters were a different brief — event flyers that needed
to read from across a room. I used high contrast color schemes, skull
motifs, and stencil type. Each variant was a different format: a
stacked vertical flyer, a horizontal layout, and a compact handbill.

The Nether album was the most design-intensive piece. It required a
cover, interior panels, and back matter with a consistent visual
thread. I built the package around a fog motif — a dark, surreal
landscape on the cover with a figure emerging from the haze, carrying
that atmosphere through the interior and back panel.

The band's logo treatment was the simplest piece and the one that saw
the most use. A white version for dark backgrounds, deployed across
print and web.
