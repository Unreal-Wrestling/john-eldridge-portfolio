Tripping Up the Basement Stairs Before Dawn is a collection of songs
and poetry by my father, John Seymour Eldridge. He passed before the
book was published. I designed the cover, set the interior, and
prepared the production files for Balboa Press, the self-publishing
imprint that printed and distributed the book.

The book collected a lifetime of writing — songs he had written across
decades, poems he had composed in his later years, and fragments that
had never been typed up. The manuscript was assembled from his papers
after his death. My role was to take that manuscript and turn it into
a published book: a cover that honored the work, an interior that was
readable and dignified, and production files that met Balboa Press's
specifications for both softcover and dust jacket hardcover editions.

The cover illustration was done by Leah Griffiths. I art-directed the
illustration, providing reference images and direction on mood and
composition, but the painting itself is hers. My work was the design
system around it — the typography, the layout, the interior, and the
production.

The book was published in both softcover and dust jacket hardcover
editions. It is available through Balboa Press and standard retail
channels.

## Cover Concept

The title — Tripping Up the Basement Stairs Before Dawn — carries a
specific image: someone moving through a dark, transitional space,
half-awake, heading somewhere before the world is up. The cover
needed to hold that image without illustrating it too literally.

I started by collecting reference photographs of basement staircases
and abandoned interiors. The Grabowsee clinic staircase — a decaying
building with a narrow, curved stairwell — became the primary
reference. I shared it with Leah Griffiths along with a second
photograph of a dim basement stairwell to establish the lighting and
mood.

Before commissioning the illustration, I built several digital
mockups. The first version was a straight photograph with the title
overlaid in a handwritten script typeface. The second refined that
composition with darker tones and better text placement. I also
produced a series of collage sketches that layered staircase imagery
with fragments of my father's handwriting and poetry text, testing
whether the cover should feel warm or cool, hopeful or melancholic.

The collage explorations pushed the direction toward something more
atmospheric than a straight photograph would allow. That is when I
brought in Leah Griffiths to create an original illustration that
could carry the mood without being literal.

## Cover Design

The final cover pairs Leah Griffiths' illustration with a two-typeface
system. The title is set in a handwritten script — Desyrel — that
echoes the personal, handwritten quality of my father's manuscripts.
The author name and subsidiary text are set in Tisa Pro, an Italian
Old Style serif that gives the cover its structural backbone without
competing with the illustration.

The illustration itself depicts a figure descending a basement
staircase in muted blue and amber tones. The stairs curve downward
into shadow. The figure is small in the frame — the staircase is the
subject, not the person. The mood is quiet and transitional, which is
what the title calls for.

I produced two production files for Balboa Press: a softcover edition
and a dust jacket hardcover edition. Both used the same cover art, but
the dust jacket required a full wraparound layout with spine text,
back panel copy, and barcode placement. The softcover was a single
piece with the cover art wrapped to the front, spine, and back.

## Typesetting & Interior

The interior needed two typefaces working together. My father's
handwritten manuscripts used a script style for poem titles, and I
wanted to carry that into the printed book. Desyrel, the same
handwritten script used on the cover, handled the poem and song
titles. Tisa Pro, a clean Italian Old Style serif, handled everything
else — chapter headings, table of contents, and body text.

I tested the pairing before committing to it. The typeface test shows
the two working together: a chapter heading in Tisa Pro, a poem title
in Desyrel, and body text in Tisa Pro. The contrast between the
structured serif and the loose script gives each page a rhythm —
formal where it needs to be, personal where it should be.

The interior was typeset in Adobe InDesign at 5.5 × 8.5 inches, the
trim size required by Balboa Press. I produced the final interior as a
print-ready PDF and the cover as separate softcover and dust jacket
InDesign files, all meeting Balboa Press's production specifications.
