Dead Kiss was a local metal band playing the Pacific Northwest
circuit. They needed a logo that could work on a T-shirt, a banner,
a CD insert, and a social media profile — the standard set of
surfaces a working band needs to cover. The name itself suggested
the visual direction: something dark, something with teeth.

I started with pencil sketches. The first round explored
hand-lettered forms — angular, aggressive, the kind of typography
that reads as metal without needing a skull attached to it. But the
skull kept coming back. The idea of replacing the dot in the "i"
with a small skull gave the wordmark its personality without
turning the whole logo into an illustration. It was a detail that
rewarded a closer look.

The sketch phase took about six weeks. Most of that time was
spent on letter proportions and the relationship between "Dead" and
"Kiss" — the two words needed to read as a single unit, not two
words stacked next to each other. The "D" and "K" needed to carry
enough weight to anchor the wordmark without overwhelming the
lowercase letters between them.

The final logo was built as a vector in Illustrator, with sharp
terminals on the letterforms that echoed the cut-and-thrust
aesthetic of metal typography without going full blackletter. The
skull in the "i" was kept simple — a few shapes, readable at small
sizes, not a detailed anatomical drawing. The logo was delivered
in three variants: full-color, solid black, and solid white, each
as a vector EPS and a PNG. The black and white variants were built
for apparel printing where a single-color pass keeps costs down.

The band used the logo on T-shirts and promotional materials
through their active years. The shirt mockup shows how the wordmark
reads at garment scale — the skull detail holds up, and the
letterforms stay legible from across a room.
