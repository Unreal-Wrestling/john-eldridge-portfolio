Beat the Ridge is a charity initiative supporting young children
fighting cancer. I was asked to design a T-shirt that the kids could
wear — something that felt tough and hopeful at the same time, not
cute, not clinical.

The name "Beat the Ridge" uses a mountain metaphor: cancer is the
ridge, and you climb it. The design needed to carry that idea without
being heavy. These are little kids wearing it. It had to feel like
something they would want to put on.

I started with a first version that established the ridge line and
text layout. The early pass was close but the illustration felt too
flat and the lettering needed more personality. I refined the ridge
into a cleaner vector illustration with more dimension and reworked
the wordmark into a bold, hand-lettered style that reads strong at
small sizes — important for a children's small chest print.

The final design is a mountain ridge in layered blues and greens with
a hand-lettered "Beat the Ridge" wordmark above it. The artwork was
delivered as print-ready vector files in AI, EPS, PNG, and JPG
formats, sized and positioned for chest placement on youth-sized
T-shirts.
