Poetry Northwest is a literary journal that has been in continuous
publication since 1959. The project for Graph 299 was to design a
cover for the Spring 2013 issue, plus explore a logo direction for
the journal's ongoing identity. The journal's editorial board
supplied the issue's poems and the photographer's image; my job was
to build a cover that honored the literary heritage while making
something a reader would pick up off a shelf.

## The Cover

The final cover pairs a striking botanical photograph with the
journal's title set in a classic serif. The photograph carries the
emotional weight; the typography stays quiet and lets the image lead.
The title treatment respects the journal's literary heritage without
feeling dated — a serif that has been in use for decades, set with
enough air around it to feel contemporary.

[[01-cover.jpg]]

I explored a second cover direction using a spherical photographic
image — a more abstract approach that treated the cover as a single
visual statement rather than a photograph with a title on top. It
was a stronger design object, but the editorial board felt the
botanical image connected more directly to the issue's themes.

[[02-cover-concept-sphere.png]]

## The Logo

Alongside the cover, I explored a logo direction that combined
photography and typography — a wordmark that could work as the
journal's ongoing identity across covers and stationery. The
concept integrates a photographic texture into the letterforms,
so the mark carries both the literary and visual identity in one
piece.

[[03-logo-concept.jpg]]

The simplified mark strips the concept down to its essential form for
use at small sizes — on a spine, a web header, or a subscription
card. The photographic texture drops away and what remains is the
letterform structure, which still reads as Poetry Northwest at a
fraction of the space.
