Flights Pub was a live music bar at 7601 Evergreen Way in Everett.
The name works two ways — beer flights and dart flights — and the
room ran on bands, a darts league, and a kitchen. Between late 2012
and early 2014 I handled the logo, a brand banner, a show poster,
and a phased rebuild of the website.

The logo came out of the backline. A pub built around live music
already has a visual language sitting on its own stage, so instead
of reaching for a beer glass or a dart, I drew the mark as an
amplifier head. I worked from real cabinets — a Blackstar HT Stage
100 for the proportions, a Blackstar ID for the way a manufacturer
wordmark sits on the grille, and an Orange TH30 for the color. The
pub name went where the badge goes, knocked out of the grille in a
rounded geometric sans, with the carry handle, corner brackets, and
a row of knobs drawn in underneath. It reads as an amp at a glance
and as a sign from across the parking lot.

The banner extended the mark into a horizontal lockup for the site
header and social covers, adding "Music, Food, Spirits" in a loose
connected script and setting the whole thing on the orange pulled
from the Orange cabinet. The script was a deliberate counterweight.
The amp is all hard edges and geometry, and the tagline needed to
soften it enough to read as hospitality rather than gear.

The website was the largest piece of the engagement. I bid it in
October 2012 as a WordPress rebuild on the PageLines framework and
delivered phase one in June 2013. The existing site had real
problems: a broken calendar, a booking form that silently failed to
send, a contact page with a redundant email table, and dead links
throughout. I rebuilt the booking form from scratch, replaced the
broken calendar with a linked ReverbNation feed and a map, and cut
the contact page down to usable location and contact information.
On the home page I remade the slider from the existing picture
library, added a promotional highlight to the sidebar, and wired in
the pub's Brown Paper Tickets account so the next featured show and
its ticket link surfaced without anyone editing a page. Four
unfinished pages — Darts, Menu, VIP, and the merch shop — I pulled
down rather than ship them half-built.

I bid phase two at $2,100 to finish the site with five more pages:
darts, schedule, prizes and teams, gallery, and food and beer. The
client came back on price and I revised it to $1,500 in June 2013.
That second phase never ran, so the site stayed at the phase one
build.

The last piece was a poster. On January 24, 2014 the pub ran a
birthday show for Mischa Kianne and Polecat Kelley with Witchburn
and Zero Down and a Brother Jack reunion. I composited it from
band-supplied assets — the hero image is Zero Down's own album art
for Looking to Start a Riot — and built the type treatment,
hierarchy, and layout around them, with the amp-head logo anchoring
the top left. It had to do the ordinary work a show poster does:
name the bands in billing order, and put the cover price, the age
limit, the door time, and the address where someone scanning it on
a wall would actually find them.
